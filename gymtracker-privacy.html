GymTracker Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 October 2026

This policy covers the GymTracker app for iPhone, iPad and Android, published by Kalikoutsi. The companion Terms of Use cover what the app does and does not promise.

The short version: GymTracker has no account, no server, and nothing to sign up for. Everything you record lives on your phone, and the only things that can ever leave it are a crash report you have switched on, a spreadsheet you have asked to export, and a routine, program or progress card you have chosen to share.

What stays on your device

Your workouts, sets, reps and loads; your exercise history; your body weight and other body metrics; your goals, programs and calendar; your companion, its levels and your awards; and every setting you choose. All of it is stored in a database on the device itself. We operate no server that receives it, we cannot see it, and we have no way to retrieve it — which also means we cannot recover it for you if you lose the device without a backup.

What we collect

Nothing, by default. GymTracker requires no account, no email address and no sign-in. We do not know how many people use the app, who they are, or what they do in it.

Analytics and tracking

The app contains no analytics SDKs, no advertising identifiers, no tracking pixels and no third-party marketing tools. Nothing you do in the app is measured or profiled, and nothing about you is shared with data brokers or advertisers. In App Store terms, the app collects no data used to track you.

Crash reporting, which is off unless you turn it on

GymTracker can send crash reports so that faults can be fixed. It is off by default and becomes active only if you switch it on yourself in Settings. You can switch it off again at any time.

When it is on, a crash — or the app freezing for several seconds — sends a report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service hosted in the European Union. The report holds the stack trace, your device model, and the OS and app version. It may also carry a short trail of the screens and buttons used just before the fault, and a random ID the crash library makes so that one device hitting the same fault twice counts once. It does not include your name, email address or any account identifier — there is no account — and it carries no workout data, no body metrics and nothing from Apple Health. There is no session replay and no usage analytics: a fault is the only thing that causes a request. Reports stay in Sentry for a limited time and are then deleted; we do not copy them anywhere else.

The app also keeps a crash log on the device itself, to show you what went wrong. That log never leaves your phone, whatever the reporting setting says.

Backups and exports

GymTracker backs itself up on its own, about five seconds after you log, change or delete a set, and whenever you tap Back up now. The last seven backups are kept inside the app, where only GymTracker can open them: the Files app does not show them, and on iPhone they are left out of iCloud backup because they can hold readings that came from Apple Health. Your phone's own backup still includes the app. Before a restore or an import, the app saves a rescue copy the same way; before a reset it takes one more backup.

Export a spreadsheet writes your training history as a file and hands it to your device's own share sheet; you choose where it goes — Files, Mail, AirDrop or anywhere else your phone offers. We operate no cloud storage and the file is never uploaded to us. Once you have sent it somewhere, what happens to it is governed by whichever service you sent it to, not by this policy.

Sharing a routine or a progress card

Share, on a routine or program you made, writes a small file holding its exercises, sets and reps, plus any exercise you made up — nothing about you and none of your workouts — and hands it to the share sheet. Older versions sent a link, which still opens the same way. Share my progress, on the Progress tab, sends a short message, or a card you can screenshot. Both happen only when you tap Share, and once something is sent it cannot be taken back.

Notifications

Reminders — rest timers, weigh-ins, check-ins and inactivity reminders — are scheduled on your device by the app itself. They do not travel through a push notification server, so we do not know whether one was sent, received or opened.

Health data

On iPhone, GymTracker never touches Apple Health until you connect it in Settings, and you can disconnect at any time. On Android there is no health-platform connection. When connected, it reads your weight and weight history, your body fat and lean mass if something measures them, your height, sex and date of birth, and the walking, running, cycling and other cardio Health already knows about; it writes the workouts and weigh-ins you record in GymTracker back to Health. Weights read from Health are shown in your weight log but never stored by the app, so they are not in its backups or its spreadsheet export. Your height, date of birth, sex, body fat and lean mass are copied into your profile while Health is connected, each marked with where it came from; they stay in the profile if you disconnect, and you can change them by hand. Because they are part of your profile, those copied values are in your backups and your spreadsheet export. GymTracker itself never sends anything read from Health anywhere.

Not medical advice

GymTracker provides general training and nutrition guidance to help you plan and track your activity. It does not assess injuries and it does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. If you are injured, ill, pregnant or recovering, talk to a doctor before starting or changing your training, and follow the advice of your doctor or physiotherapist over anything the app suggests. Talk to a doctor or dietitian before making changes to what you eat that could affect your health. The app applies more cautious limits where it can, but it cannot know your medical history, and you use its suggestions at your own judgement.

Your rights over your data

Because your data never reaches us, you exercise these rights directly in the app rather than by writing to us:

  • Access and portability — Settings › Data › Export a spreadsheet gives you your log, profile, goals, schedule, settings and the things you made, in a format other software can open.
  • Erasure — Settings › Data › Reset all data deletes your log, profile and settings; it takes one backup first, which is one of the seven the app keeps, so newer backups replace it in time. Deleting the app deletes everything, backups included.
  • Rectification — every value you have entered can be edited in the app.

If you have questions we can actually answer, write to gymtracker_support@kalikoutsi.com. Please note that we cannot look up, restore or delete your data for you, because we do not have it.

Children

GymTracker is not directed at children. It collects nothing from anyone, of any age, beyond the opt-in crash reports described above. Where the app gives guidance about eating that depends on age, it applies more cautious limits for anyone under eighteen.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of this page is updated. Continued use of the app after a change constitutes acceptance of the updated policy.
